# Information design and patient communication

Patient-facing material should help the reader understand:
1. what service is being discussed;
2. where to go;
3. what to bring;
4. what to do;
5. what will happen next;
6. who to contact;
7. what to do if symptoms worsen or plans change.

Use plain language, short scannable headings, one action per step, approved patient-friendly service names and clear contact/next-step information.

## Public notices
Use the approved public-notice template where available. Structure:
- SJH identity;
- notice title;
- affected service or audience;
- date/effective date;
- essential message in plain language;
- action required;
- contact or next step.

## Patient information sheets
Use approved SJH visual identity, clear headings, step-by-step instructions, 12 pt minimum body text for print, and current contact details. If the material routinely needs staff explanation, revise it for clarity.

Do not invent clinical instructions. Edit approved clinical content for clarity only when meaning is preserved; route clinically substantive changes to the appropriate clinical authority.
